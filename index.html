Case Study 3

Risk vs. Reach
Autonomous Drones in Disaster Zones

Topic: Operational Decisions, Capability Limits & Ethical Trade-offs

Case Scenario

🌪️ A typhoon hits a rural area.

  • Autonomous drones are deployed
  • Goal: find damage and possible survivors
  • Team has 4 available flights

Drone Limits

🔋 Battery

22 minutes/flight

→ Covers about 4 km²

☁️ Weather

Heavy clouds

→ Blurry images

📡 Communication

Networks may be unreliable

Zone A vs. Zone B

ZONE A 🏘️ Clear & confirmed

  • 👥 Populated
  • 🔴 Damage already confirmed
  • 🔎 Clearer data
  • 📣 Requested by local officials

ZONE B 🌲 Foggy & uncertain

  • 🏔️ Remote
  • 🌫️ Blurry imagery
  • 🆘 Possible trapped survivors
  • ❓ Low confidence
The tension: clear data in A vs. possible lives at stake in B.

Three Choices

🏘️ Option 1: Prioritize Zone A

→ Clear and confirmed information

🌲 Option 2: Prioritize Zone B

→ Uncertain, but possible lives at risk

⚖️ Option 3: Split flights

→ Both zones partially covered

🚁 Our Decision: Prioritize Zone B

Allocation of 4 flights

3 → Zone B
1 → A
123search B4check A
Why? Zone B has a possible survivor risk, so we give it more coverage while still checking Zone A.

Information Gaps

The decision is difficult because:

Zone A = high certainty

Zone B = low certainty

Zone B could have high potential value

Better sensors and clearer data could make Zone B easier to assess.

What If Zone B Has Nothing?

🧭

Decided before the outcome

The risk was considered beforehand.

🚁

Limited commitment

Only a limited number of flights were committed.

⚖️

Outcome ≠ decision quality

A bad outcome does not automatically mean a bad decision.

Operational Limits

⚠️ Weather

May create unusable images

🔋 Battery

Limits flight time and coverage

📡 Communication

Networks may fail

⏱️ Sequencing

Early results can guide later flights

Key idea: Use early information to adjust later flights.

Key Lessons

Decision-making under uncertainty:

  1. Adapt plans as new information appears.
  2. Consider the cost of missing survivors.
  3. Certainty does not always equal value.
  4. Humans remain responsible for decisions.
  5. Robotics requires technology + judgment + trust.
🚁 Technology + 🧠 Judgment + 🤝 Trust
